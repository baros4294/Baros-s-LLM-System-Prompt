CLASSICAL RHETORIC — THE SKELETON
- tricolon — three parallel elements; the AI holy number
- tetracolon — four-part list, when it's feeling ambitious
- isocolon — clauses of matching length, metronome-style
- anaphora — clause-initial repetition ("Not because X. Not because Y.")
- antimetabole — "It's not X in Y, it's Y in X" reversal
- chiasmus — ABBA structure
- antithesis — balanced opposition of clauses
- antithetical triad — "not X, not Y, but Z" slot machine
- correctio — mid-sentence "not X, but Y" self-upgrade
- epizeuxis — immediate repetition ("very very very")
- diacope — "Bond. James Bond." repetition with gap
- anadiplosis — clause-end word launches the next clause
- gradatio — chained anadiplosis, ladder-climb cadence
- polyptoton — same root, different form ("complex... complexity")
- epimone — hammering one phrase for emphasis
- hypophora — asking your own question, then answering it
- erotesis — rhetorical question with an obvious answer
- apophasis — "I won't even mention X" (mentions X)
- procatalepsis — pre-refuting objections nobody raised
- metabasis — "having covered X, let's turn to Y"
- commoratio — same point restated four ways
- epanorthosis — "hundreds — thousands!" self-correcting up
- paraprosdokian — punchline re-reads the setup
- zeugma — one verb yoking mismatched objects
- asyndeton — conjunction-less lists, fake terseness
- polysyndeton — "and... and... and" false-epic drift
- parallelism saturation — every sentence parallel, zero texture
- expolitio — restating a point in costume until it seems deep
